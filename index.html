Featured · Desktop software

Invenfloor

Most inventory software gives you a list and expects you to remember the rest. This one asks where things are. You draw the floor plan, put containers in the rooms, and file items into the containers, so "where are the spare filters" has an answer you can point at.

Try the room

A working slice of it, running the same projection, the same rule for which walls get drawn and the same test for whether a container fits. Three containers and three items, then it stops.

Drag a container · click one to see inside

Draw the room, then fill it

Rooms are polygons, not rectangles, so an L-shaped garage is an L-shaped garage. Containers snap inside them and cannot be dragged through a wall.

The parts that were actually hard

Which walls to draw. Only the ones facing away from you. Testing that against the room's center works for a rectangle and is exactly backwards for the notch in an L, so each wall gets an outward normal worked out from the polygon's winding direction.

A container in a divot. A room shaped around a fireplace had a notch you could drop a container into, because "is it inside the outline" was answered by four conditions and only two of them were written.

Halving the height presets broke every old save. A stored 60 meant medium before and tall after, and no default can tell those apart, so save files carry a version and get migrated on load.

Picking a tier crashed the app with no traceback. Qt frees a panel the instant you replace it, and the dropdown was asking for its own panel to be rebuilt from inside its own click handler.

Games that run on economies

Two I am willing to put my name on. Both are economies first: what a player earns, what it costs them, and whether the numbers still hold up an hour in.

Supermarket Nightmare

Co-op survival built around a shopping list. You are sent for groceries and the store does not want you to finish. Two currencies, consumable power-ups you have to decide when to spend, and a Dark Market shop, over a HUD rebuilt so a player being chased can still read it.

Idle / Story

Cosmic Catcher

An idle game with a story underneath it. Catch stars, mine them for fragments, and feed a Star Engine that keeps earning while you are logged off, which means the whole economy has to stay balanced against time rather than against clicks.
